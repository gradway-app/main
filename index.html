The founder

Built by a first-generation Bulldog who wished this existed

Terence Williams grew up in Oakland, Mississippi — a small town that gave its name, years later, to the software company he'd go on to found. He arrived at Mississippi State University as a first-generation college student and an electrical engineering major, and like most freshmen, spent his first weeks turned around on a campus that felt enormous.

During a First Year Experience course built to teach incoming students the basics of iPhone programming, Williams found the fix he'd been looking for. He kept building after the class ended, and by his senior year he'd shipped Bully Walk: a simple, fast way for students, parents, and visitors to find their way around Mississippi State.

"It's something I wish I had in my first years around campus." — Terence Williams, Mississippi State University News, 2016

The recognition came quickly. Apple selected Williams for one of 350 scholarships to its 2016 Worldwide Developers Conference. Judges at MSU's College of Business named Bully Walk runner-up in the E-Week growth-startup competition, and students voted it the People's Choice winner. Per GradWay's own App Store listing, the app also picked up a White House "Local Frontier" nomination that same year.

Williams didn't stop at graduation. He founded Nobility Tech, kept rebuilding the product through a decade of iOS updates, a pandemic, and two rebrands, and today runs it through his company, Oakland Software Co. — named, fittingly, for the town where the whole idea started.

The road here

Nearly a decade. One mission.

GradWay's growth wasn't a straight line — it was a decade of small, deliberate rebuilds, each one keeping the same idea: give people one reliable way to find their way around campus.

2016
Bully Walk launches at Mississippi State
Terence Williams ships the first version as a senior. It wins Apple's WWDC scholarship, MSU's E-Week People's Choice award, and a White House Local Frontier nomination in the same year.
2016–2019
A campus staple takes shape
Bully Walk grows to 34,000 downloads against a student body of roughly 22,600, adding student portal login, dining data, and athletics info along the way.
2020–2021
Built for a campus in crisis
Pandemic-era updates add campus testing data and safety information. By fall 2021, usage hits 100,000 semester opens, with the average student opening the app six times a day.
2023–2024
Rebuilt from the ground up
The app is re-architected for scale and re-launched as "College Town," with a redesigned in-app navigation experience built to support more than one university.
2025–2026
GradWay goes national
The platform relaunches as GradWay — College Made Easy, now live at more than 15 colleges and universities, carrying a 4.8-star rating across 1,300-plus reviews into its tenth year.
The product today

One app for everything between the front gate and graduation

GradWay bundles the tools students, staff, and visiting families actually reach for during a semester — not just a map.

Turn-by-turn campus navigation

Find any building, lot, or landmark, and get walking or driving directions in seconds.

Smart search

ATMs, coffee shops, pharmacies, bookstores, parking — searchable the moment you land on a new campus.

Class schedules & directions

Manage a weekly schedule, save quick notes, and get an estimated arrival time for the next class.

Dining & meal plans

Emergency services

Announcements, event calendars, and athletics schedules, ticketing included.

For editors & press

Media boilerplate

GradWay — College Made Easy is a campus-life app that gives students, staff, and visitors one place to navigate campus, manage class schedules, check dining and academic calendars, and reach emergency services. Originally launched in 2016 as "Bully Walk" by then–Mississippi State University student Terence Williams, the app has spent nearly a decade in continuous development and is now live at more than 15 colleges and universities nationwide. GradWay is a product of Oakland Software Co.

Terence Williams is a first-generation college student from Oakland, Mississippi, and an Apple WWDC 2016 scholarship recipient. He continues to lead GradWay's development.
